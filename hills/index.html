British hill lists

Every list in one place, with heights, grid references and a free checklist.

Britain has no single list of its hills. Instead there are a dozen or more, each drawn up by a different person for a different reason — some by height, some by how far a hill drops before the next one rises, and one simply because Alfred Wainwright liked the look of it. WayMark tracks 16 of them across 2,531 summits, so a hill that counts twice only has to be marked once.

Which list should you start with?

If you walk in the Lake District, the 214 Wainwrights are the obvious first challenge, and the Birketts are the natural next one. In Scotland it is the Munros, then the Corbetts and Grahams. In Wales, the Hewitts or the Welsh 3000s. In the Peak District, the Ethels. And if you want one list that covers the whole of Britain regardless of height, it is the Marilyns.

Common questions

How many hill lists are there in Britain?

There is no official count, because anyone can compile one. WayMark tracks the 16 best-known lists, covering 2,531 distinct summits across England, Scotland, Wales and Northern Ireland.

What is hill bagging?

Hill bagging is the practice of working through a list of hills and recording each one you reach the summit of. It gives shape to a walking year and takes you to places you would never otherwise think to go.

What is the difference between a Hewitt and a Nuttall?

Both cover England and Wales above 2,000 feet. A Hewitt needs 30 metres of drop on all sides; a Nuttall needs only 15. That is why there are more Nuttalls — the gentler rule lets in subsidiary tops the Hewitt list leaves out.

Do I need to pay to track my hills?

No. WayMark is free. It runs in a web browser on any phone or computer, there is nothing to install, and every list on this page is included.

Can one hill be on more than one list?

Frequently. Scafell Pike is a Wainwright, a Hewitt, a Nuttall, a Marilyn, a Birkett and one of the National Three Peaks. WayMark counts it once and credits it to every list it belongs to.
