1. Privacy policy

WayMark is a walking log for the hills of Britain. This policy explains what information it handles, where that information goes, and what you can do about it. The short version: everything lives on your own device unless you sign in; there are no adverts, no analytics and no tracking; and the only people who ever see your log are the friends you choose.

1.1 Who is responsible

Joe Sullivan, an individual in the UK, is the data controller for information processed through WayMark. WayMark is a free, personal project and is not a company.

1.2 Using WayMark without signing in

Nothing you enter leaves your device. Your marked hills, dates, notes, photos, journal entries and routes are stored in your browser's local storage on the device you are using. Nobody, including Joe, can see them. If you clear your browser data they are gone, which is why Settings offers a text backup.

1.3 If you sign in with Google

Signing in is optional. It lets your log follow you between devices and lets you share with friends. When you sign in:

  • Google tells WayMark your Google account's user ID, display name and profile picture. Google's own privacy policy governs the sign-in itself.
  • Your log (hills, dates, notes, photos, journal, routes, settings) is copied to a database run by Google (Firebase / Cloud Firestore) under a record only your account can read. Google may store and process it on servers outside the UK; Google commits to the UK and EU standard contractual clauses for those transfers.
  • A public card with your chosen display name, picture, handle and total number of hills is created so friends can find you. Nothing else is public.

1.4 What friends and leaderboards can see

  • Friends see your name, picture, handle and totals, and any journal entry you deliberately post to them, including its photos.
  • Leaderboards show your name, picture and number of hills to the other members of that board. If you switch on "Share my dates, notes and photos" for a board, members of that board can also open those. You can switch this off at any time. The "WayMark board" is open to everyone using the app; only join it if you are happy for any WayMark user to see what you have chosen to share.
  • Photos of other people: only share pictures of people who are happy to be shared.

1.5 Requests the app makes to other services

To draw maps, find places and forecast weather, WayMark asks other services for data. Each request necessarily carries your IP address and the part of the map you are looking at (or the hill or place you searched for). None of these services receive your name, your log or your account details.

1.6 Location

WayMark only asks for your location when you tap "I am here — verify with GPS" on a hill. It uses the fix once, to check you are near the summit, and records the time, the distance from the summit and the accuracy of the fix. It does not record or store your coordinates and never reads your location in the background.

1.7 What WayMark does not do

No analytics, no advertising, no tracking pixels, no third-party cookies set by WayMark, no selling or sharing of data with anyone, no profiling, no email marketing.

1.8 How long data is kept

On your device: until you delete it or clear your browser. In the cloud: until you delete it. Settings → Account has a "Delete my account and cloud data" button which removes your log, your public card, your friendships, your leaderboard rows, your posts and your sign-in from Google's servers. Copies of photos attached to posts you shared, and reactions or comments other people left on them, may take a little longer to disappear.

1.9 Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask to see the personal data held about you, have it corrected or deleted, object to or restrict its processing, and receive a copy in a portable form (Settings → Backup gives you this instantly). You also have the right to complain to the Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

1.10 Legal basis

Storing and syncing your log is necessary to provide the service you asked for (performance of a contract). Sharing with friends and leaderboards happens only with your consent, which you can withdraw in the app. The requests to map and weather services are made in the legitimate interest of drawing the map you asked to see.

1.11 Children

WayMark is not intended for children under 13, and Google does not allow under-13s to hold their own accounts. If you are under 18, ask a parent or guardian before signing in or sharing with friends.

1.12 Changes

If this policy changes in a way that matters, the date at the top will change and the app will say so.

2.2 The hills are dangerous; you are responsible

WayMark is not a safety tool. Weather shown in the app comes from a computer model and the figure for "wind at the top" is an estimate for a pressure level, not a measurement on the summit. Routes are drawn over OpenStreetMap data and may follow paths that do not exist on the ground, are unsafe in the conditions, or are not public rights of way. Places such as car parks and pubs are community-contributed and may be wrong or out of date.

3. Cookies and storage on your device

This storage is strictly necessary for the service you asked for, so no consent banner is required under the UK Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, and none is shown.
